# IT Helpdesk Service Levels and Escalation Guide

## 1. Purpose and scope

This guide explains how to contact the IT Helpdesk of Meridian Digital Services ("Meridian"), how incidents and requests are prioritised, the response and resolution targets for each priority, and how issues are escalated when targets are at risk. It applies to all employees and contractors who use Meridian technology.

## 2. How to contact the Helpdesk

The preferred channel is the ServiceHub portal, where users can log a ticket, track progress and search the knowledge base. Users can also email helpdesk@meridian.example or use the chat widget in ServiceHub. For urgent issues, particularly any P1 or P2 incident, users should call the Helpdesk on extension 5555. The telephone line and the P1 and P2 service are available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week through our follow-the-sun support model. P3 and P4 tickets are handled during regional business hours.

## 3. Incident and request types

An incident is an unplanned interruption or reduction in the quality of a service, such as a laptop that will not start or an application that is down. A service request is a request for something standard, such as new software, a new mailbox or a permissions change. A how-to question is a request for information. Users should check the ServiceHub knowledge base first, because many common questions are answered there.

## 4. Priority levels and service targets

Priority is set from the impact on the business and the number of people affected, not from how senior the person raising the ticket is.

P1 - Critical. An enterprise-wide or client-facing outage, a security incident, or a complete loss of a critical service. Response within 15 minutes. Resolution target of 4 hours. Updates every 30 minutes.

P2 - High. A team or department cannot work normally, or a critical service is seriously degraded with no workaround. Response within 1 hour. Resolution target of 8 business hours. Updates every 2 hours.

P3 - Medium. A single user is affected, or a service is degraded and a workaround exists. Response within 4 business hours. Resolution target of 3 business days.

P4 - Low. How-to questions, standard service requests and minor issues. Response within 1 business day. Resolution target of 5 business days.

Targets are measured from the time the ticket is logged until the service is restored, not until the ticket is closed. Time spent waiting for the user does not count against the target.

## 5. Escalation

5.1 Support tiers. Tickets start with Level 1, the Helpdesk. If Level 1 cannot resolve the ticket, it passes to Level 2, the specialist teams. Complex problems then go to Level 3, the engineering and vendor teams. The IT Service Delivery Manager owns the final escalation.

5.2 Automatic escalation. A ticket escalates automatically to the next level and the Service Delivery Manager is notified when 75% of the resolution target has passed without resolution.

5.3 User-requested escalation. A user can ask for escalation at any time after the response target has been missed. They can click Escalate in ServiceHub, reply to the ticket with the word Escalate, or call extension 5555. Raising a priority without a valid business reason is not permitted; the Helpdesk may reset the priority.

5.4 Management escalation. If an issue has not been resolved after it has been escalated, the user's manager can ask the Service Delivery Manager for a review at servicedelivery@meridian.example.

## 6. Major incidents and communication

A P1 incident triggers the major incident process. A Major Incident Manager is appointed within 15 minutes, a bridge call is opened, and updates are posted on the ServiceHub status page and sent by email every 30 minutes until service is restored. A post-incident review is held within 5 working days. If a P1 incident affects a client's service, it is also treated as a Sev 1 business incident under OPS-004, and the Account Director informs the client.

## 7. Security incidents and phishing

Suspected phishing emails should be reported with the Report Phish button in the email client. A suspected account compromise, malware infection or lost device is treated as P1, and users should call the Helpdesk straight away rather than raise a ticket online. Lost or stolen devices must be reported within 2 hours of discovery (IT-003).

## 8. Hardware support

Hardware faults are logged as P3 unless the user cannot work at all. If a laptop cannot be repaired on the same day, the Helpdesk issues a loaner laptop on the next business day. Walk-up support is available at the larger offices from 09:00 to 17:00 on working days.

## 9. Reopening and feedback

A resolved ticket can be reopened by the user within 5 working days if the problem has come back. After a ticket is closed, the user receives a short satisfaction survey. The Helpdesk reviews every response with a score of 2 or lower out of 5 and follows up within 2 working days. Satisfaction scores and SLA performance are published monthly on ServiceHub.

## 10. Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to get help with an urgent outage? Call extension 5555. How long do I wait for a P1? Response within 15 minutes and a target resolution of 4 hours. Who gets told if my ticket is late? The Service Delivery Manager, automatically at 75% of the resolution time. My laptop is broken; can I get a replacement? A loaner will be issued the next business day if it cannot be repaired the same day.
