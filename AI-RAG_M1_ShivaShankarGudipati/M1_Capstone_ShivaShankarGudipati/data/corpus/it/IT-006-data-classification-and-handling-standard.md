# Data Classification and Handling Standard

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard tells everyone at Meridian Digital Services ("Meridian") how to classify information and how to handle it at each classification. It protects client data, employee data and Meridian's own intellectual property, and it supports our legal and contractual obligations. It applies to information in any form: documents, email, chat, databases, source code, paper and conversation.

## 2. The four classification levels

Public: information approved for release to anyone, for example press releases, published marketing material and the careers site. Disclosure causes no harm.

Internal: information meant for employees and contractors, for example policies, organisation charts, internal announcements and training material. Disclosure outside Meridian would be inappropriate but not seriously damaging. This is the default classification for anything that has not been labelled.

Confidential: information whose disclosure could harm Meridian or a client, for example client contracts, proposals and pricing, non-public financial results, source code and architecture documents.

Restricted: information whose disclosure could cause serious harm to individuals, Meridian or clients, or breach the law. Examples are personal data (including names combined with identifiers), employee salary, ratings, health and disciplinary records, credentials and keys, security vulnerabilities and unannounced mergers or acquisitions.

## 3. Labelling

Document authors must apply a label using the labelling toolbar in the office suite. Emails and chat messages inherit the label of any attachment. Unlabelled information is treated as Internal. If you are unsure which level applies, choose the higher one and ask the Information Security Team.

## 4. Handling rules

4.1 Internal. May be stored and shared on approved Meridian systems. Must not be posted on public sites or sent to personal email accounts.

4.2 Confidential. Must be encrypted in transit and at rest, shared only on a need-to-know basis and only through approved tools. External sharing needs an approved agreement, such as a non-disclosure agreement, in place. Confidential material must not be copied to personal devices or storage.

4.3 Restricted. All the rules for Confidential apply, plus: access is logged and reviewed; the data may be sent outside Meridian only by an approved secure transfer method with prior approval from the data owner; removable media is not allowed; the information must not be entered into any AI tool unless a specific system has been approved by the Privacy Office and Information Security (see IT-004); and printed copies must be locked away and shredded after use.

## 5. Personal data about colleagues

Information about individual colleagues, including their salary, performance ratings, health, disciplinary history and personal contact details, is Restricted. HR holds this information. Employees can view their own records on PeopleHub. Line managers can see only what the HR process gives them about their direct reports. Anyone who needs information about another employee for a business reason must ask their HR Business Partner, who will decide whether it can be shared. Employees must not ask colleagues, managers or systems to reveal another person's Restricted data.

## 6. Client data and production data

Client data stays in the regions and systems that the client contract approves. Production data must not be copied to development or test environments unless it has been masked or anonymised and the client has agreed in writing. Employees must follow any stricter handling rules in the client contract.

## 7. Reporting breaches

An actual or suspected data breach must be reported to Security Operations and the IT Helpdesk immediately, and no later than 2 hours after discovery. A breach includes the loss or theft of a device that holds Confidential or Restricted data, an email sent to the wrong recipient, and unauthorised access to a system. Employees must not try to investigate or hide the incident themselves. Where personal data is involved, the Privacy Office decides whether the law requires notification of a regulator, which in many countries must happen within 72 hours of Meridian becoming aware of the breach, and whether clients and affected individuals need to be told. Clients are told in line with their contract, normally through the Account Director (see OPS-004).

## 8. Retention and disposal

Business records are kept for the periods in the records schedule. Employee records are kept for 7 years after separation. Project records are kept for 7 years after project closure. Email is kept for 3 years and chat messages for 1 year. When the retention period ends, information is deleted securely. Legal holds override the schedule, and employees must not delete anything covered by a hold.

## 9. Data subject requests

Individuals can ask what personal data Meridian holds about them, and can ask for it to be corrected or deleted. Requests must be forwarded to the Privacy Office at privacy@meridian.example on the day they are received. The Privacy Office responds within 30 days.

## 10. Responsibilities

Every employee is responsible for classifying and handling information correctly. Managers make sure their teams complete the annual information security training. Data owners decide who may access their data. The Information Security and Privacy Office sets the standard, monitors compliance and investigates breaches. Breaches of this standard are handled under HR-002.

## 11. Frequently asked questions

What level is a client contract? Confidential. What level is an employee's salary? Restricted. What do I do if I emailed a spreadsheet to the wrong person? Report it within 2 hours to Security Operations. How long are project records kept? 7 years after closure. Can I copy production data to my test environment? Not unless it has been masked and the client has agreed.
