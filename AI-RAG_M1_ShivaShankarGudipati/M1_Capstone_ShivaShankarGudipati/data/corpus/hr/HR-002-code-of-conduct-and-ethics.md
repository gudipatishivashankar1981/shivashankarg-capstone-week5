# Code of Conduct and Ethics

## 1. Purpose

The Code of Conduct describes the behaviour Meridian Digital Services ("Meridian") expects from every employee, contractor and director. It protects our clients, our colleagues and our reputation. Where this Code and local law differ, the stricter rule applies. Managers are responsible for making sure their teams understand the Code and for acting when they see a breach.

## 2. Our principles

Four principles guide every decision. We act with integrity: we are honest with clients, colleagues and regulators. We treat people with respect: we do not tolerate harassment, bullying or discrimination. We protect information: we handle client, employee and company data according to the classification rules in IT-006. We follow the rules: we comply with laws, contracts and Meridian policies, and we ask for guidance when the right course of action is unclear.

## 3. Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest exists when a personal relationship or financial interest could influence, or appear to influence, a business decision. Common examples are a close family member working for a supplier or competitor, a financial interest of more than 1% in a vendor or competitor, and a directorship in another company.

Every employee must complete the annual conflict-of-interest declaration on PeopleHub by 30 April each year. Employees must also update their declaration within 10 working days of any change in circumstances. Employees who sit on a procurement evaluation panel must declare any conflict with a bidder before the evaluation starts and withdraw from the evaluation if a conflict exists (see OPS-002).

## 4. Gifts and hospitality

4.1 Gifts received. Employees may accept a gift worth up to USD 50 per giver per calendar year, provided it is not cash or a cash equivalent, has not been solicited, and is not offered while a procurement or bid involving the giver is open. Gifts worth more than USD 50 must be politely declined. If declining would cause serious offence, the employee must hand the gift to the Ethics Office within 5 working days of receiving it. The Ethics Office decides whether to return it, donate it or raffle it for charity.

4.2 Hospitality received. Meals and events offered by third parties may be accepted when they are occasional, business-related and worth less than USD 100 per person per event.

4.3 Gifts given. Gifts and hospitality offered to clients are governed by OPS-001 and must be recorded in the gift register.

4.4 Public officials. No gift, payment or hospitality of any value may be offered to a public official without written approval from Legal.

## 5. Outside employment

Employees need the written approval of their manager and HR Business Partner before taking any paid outside work or a role in another business. Approval will not be given for work with a Meridian competitor or client, or work that would interfere with the employee's duties or involve Meridian time, equipment or confidential information.

## 6. Confidentiality and social media

Employees must not disclose client names, project details, financial results or other non-public information outside Meridian. On social media, employees should make clear that their views are personal. They must not post screenshots of internal systems, client environments or internal chat.

## 7. Harassment and discrimination

Meridian has zero tolerance for harassment, bullying, victimisation or discrimination on any ground. Employees who experience or witness such behaviour can report it to their HR Business Partner, to the Internal Complaints Committee, or through the Ethics Hotline. Reports are investigated promptly and confidentially. Employees who raise a concern in good faith are protected from retaliation.

## 8. Speaking up

The Ethics Hotline is run by an independent third party. It is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and accepts anonymous reports by phone, web form or email. The Ethics Office acknowledges every report within 2 working days and aims to close investigations within 45 days. Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern, or who takes part in an investigation, is itself a serious breach of this Code.

## 9. Training

All new joiners must complete the Code of Conduct training within 30 days of their start date. All employees must repeat the training every 12 months. Completion is tracked on PeopleHub, and managers receive a report of overdue training each month. Employees with overdue training may be refused access to client production environments.

## 10. Consequences of a breach

Breaches are handled through the disciplinary process. Depending on severity, outcomes range from a verbal or written warning to termination of employment. Serious breaches such as fraud, bribery, harassment and deliberate disclosure of confidential data may lead to immediate termination and, where appropriate, referral to law enforcement. Unauthorised absence of more than 3 consecutive working days is treated as absence without leave and can lead to disciplinary action (see HR-001).

## 11. Where to get advice

Employees who are unsure whether something is permitted can email ethics@meridian.example before acting. Asking early is always preferred to correcting a problem later. Managers should not try to resolve potential breaches themselves; they should contact the Ethics Office or their HR Business Partner as soon as they become aware of a concern.
