# Performance Management and Appraisal Guide

## 1. Purpose

This guide explains how Meridian Digital Services ("Meridian") sets goals, gives feedback, rates performance and makes decisions on promotion and pay. It applies to all permanent employees. Its aim is a fair, evidence-based process in which employees know what is expected of them and how they are doing throughout the year, not only at year-end.

## 2. The annual performance cycle

The performance year runs from 1 January to 31 December.

Goal setting: employees agree their goals with their manager by 31 January. Every employee must set at least one development goal in addition to business goals.

Mid-year check-in: a documented conversation between employee and manager must take place by 31 July. Goals can be revised at this point if priorities have changed.

Year-end review: employees complete a self-assessment in December, and managers write their assessment in January. Ratings are calibrated across teams in February and finalised by 28 February.

Compensation outcomes: salary increases and bonuses linked to the cycle take effect on 1 April.

## 3. Eligibility for a rating

Employees who joined Meridian on or before 30 September of the performance year receive a rating in that year's cycle. Employees who joined on or after 1 October are not rated in that cycle. They are evaluated in the next cycle, and their manager gives informal feedback in the meantime. Employees on approved long leave are rated on the basis of the period they worked, with the HR Business Partner's guidance.

## 4. Continuous feedback

Feedback is not saved up for the formal review. Employees and managers should record feedback on PeopleHub throughout the year. Colleagues can also give feedback to each other. For employees at grade L6 and above, a 360-degree feedback exercise with at least 6 reviewers is added to the year-end process.

## 5. The rating scale

Ratings use a five-point scale:

1 - Needs Improvement: performance is well below expectations.
2 - Partially Meets: some goals are met, but there are significant gaps.
3 - Meets: all core goals are delivered to the expected standard.
4 - Exceeds: goals are consistently exceeded.
5 - Outstanding: exceptional impact beyond the role, recognised across the organisation.

Meridian does not use a forced distribution. Calibration panels do, however, review ratings of 5 closely and expect roughly 10% of employees or fewer to receive them. Managers must give written evidence for every rating of 1, 2, 4 or 5.

## 6. Promotion

To be nominated for promotion, an employee must have spent at least 18 months in their current grade, have a rating of 4 or above in the most recent cycle and a rating of 3 or above in the cycle before it. The manager nominates the employee, and a calibration panel decides on the basis of evidence of sustained impact at the next grade. Meeting the minimum criteria does not guarantee promotion.

## 7. Performance Improvement Plans

An employee is placed on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) if they receive a rating of 1, or a rating of 2 in two consecutive cycles. A PIP lasts 90 days. It sets out specific, measurable expectations and the support that will be provided, and it includes check-ins between the employee and manager every two weeks. At the end of the PIP, the manager and HR Business Partner decide whether the employee has met the expectations, the PIP should be extended once by up to 30 days, or the employment should be reviewed. Employees on a PIP can use the confidential Employee Assistance Programme described in HR-005.

## 8. Appeals

An employee who disagrees with their rating can appeal in writing to their HR Business Partner within 10 working days of receiving it. A review panel that was not involved in the original rating decides the appeal within 15 working days of receiving it. The panel's decision is final. Employees are protected from retaliation for making an appeal (see HR-002).

## 9. Remote and hybrid employees

Remote and hybrid employees are assessed on outcomes against the same goals and standards as office-based employees (see HR-003). Managers must not treat office attendance beyond the agreed pattern as evidence of performance.

## 10. Confidentiality of ratings

Ratings, calibration discussions and compensation outcomes are Restricted information under IT-006. An employee can see their own rating and feedback on PeopleHub. Managers can see the ratings of their direct reports only. Ratings of other employees must not be shared, discussed or requested outside the HR and calibration process. HR Business Partners can answer questions about the process but cannot disclose another person's rating.

## 11. Frequently asked questions

When will I know my rating? Ratings are finalised by 28 February and shared in the conversation with your manager. When will my pay change? Outcomes take effect on 1 April. Can I be rated 5 in my first year? Yes, if you joined on or before 30 September and the evidence supports it. Where do I record my goals? On PeopleHub under Performance > My Goals.
